Industries & Sectors

Residential, commercial and industrial solar energy solutions — each designed around the way that sector actually consumes electricity.

Sector Expertise

Different Sectors, Different Energy Profiles

A family home has a light, irregular daytime load. A shopping complex runs refrigeration, lighting, air conditioning and lifts for long hours. A manufacturing unit has a heavy, continuous demand profile that needs to be matched very differently.

Pro Solar works across all three, and designs the array, the protection and the storage around the operating pattern of that specific sector rather than reusing a standard template.

Sector 01

Residential / Domestic

Homes and residences installing solar to reduce monthly electricity bills, protect against load shedding and run essential appliances for a few hours when the supply fails.

Typical Requirements
  • Daytime loads: fans, lights, TV, refrigerator, washing machine
  • Backup priorities: lights, fans, internet, kitchen appliances
  • Air conditioning in summer and water pumping
  • Avoided overload of the single-phase supply
Recommended Approach

An on-grid system where supply is reliable, or a hybrid system with a modest battery where outages are frequent and evening use is high.

Sector 02

Commercial

Shops, offices, shopping complexes, businesses and commercial facilities. These premises usually operate long hours, pay commercial tariffs, and lose revenue every time power is interrupted.

Typical Requirements
  • Extended operating hours with high daytime air conditioning load
  • Refrigeration, display lighting, POS and office equipment
  • Three-phase supply where connected load is high
  • Peak-hour tariff reduction and backup for critical circuits
Recommended Approach

On-grid generation sized against the daytime load, combined with a hybrid battery system where revenue depends on staying open.

Discuss A Commercial System
Sector 03

Industrial

Factories, manufacturing units and industrial facilities. Industrial solar is an engineering problem first: production continuity, machine loads, power quality and the cost of grid power at scale.

Typical Requirements
  • Heavy three-phase motors and continuous process loads
  • Production continuity and protection against supply interruption
  • Peak demand charges and overall energy cost reduction
  • Land availability for ground-mount arrays or large rooftop arrays
Recommended Approach

High-capacity ground-mount or rooftop generation with battery energy storage to smooth demand, support critical machinery and reduce dependence on the grid.
